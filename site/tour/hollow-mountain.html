Direct The Hollow Mountain.

How do you film the missing part of a mountain? Follow a creative brief through two pitches, a real terrain download and one marked camera revision. Watch the finished 30-second film with its original score.

Read and copy the directing prompts

Ask for two treatments

The Hollow Mountain. Pitch two visual and sonic treatments for a 30-second film about Mount St. Helens. Make the viewer feel the scale of what is missing. Wait for my choice.

Choose the walker and source the terrain

One Pair of Boots. Put a lone amber walker inside a real crater. Find the Mount St. Helens Skadi elevation tile on AWS, download it and save a cropped height grid and its source in media. Show me the data before building.

Direct the first cut

Build One Pair of Boots as a 30-second, 1280 x 720 film at 24 fps. Use the Landscape rig and original-score.wav. Start six metres behind the walker, then pull back through the northern breach to five kilometres. Let the title arrive over the wide shot. Check the render.

Give a note on the marked interval

I lose the walker here. Stay within 12 metres through 13 seconds. Then climb into the wide shot on the musical swell. Bring the score up to -2 dB.

Inside the session

  1. A single walker gives the crater a human scale.

  2. Ask for two visual and sonic treatments before choosing a shot.

  3. A photographic seam or a retreating walker? Compare the ideas.

  4. Follow the live download command from the AWS tile to the sampled surface.

  5. Give the agent a terrain renderer, a score and a camera intention.

  6. Watch where the walker becomes too small to follow.

  7. Mark seconds eight to thirteen and describe what the viewer should still see.

  8. The agent measures the score and changes the camera distance curve.

  9. Two synchronized cuts make the change in scale visible.

  10. Thirty seconds of continuous motion, with wind, footsteps and an original score.
